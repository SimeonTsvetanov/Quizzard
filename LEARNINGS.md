- 2026-11-22 · Moni on the live app: (1) "Teams copied ✓" stayed over the menu he opened right after Copy; (2) every share must say Quizzard with the Quizzard link (Scores still said GG Counter, Share app sent only the tagline); (3) the menus were not filled in for a suite — Help had nothing about Teams or Scores, Settings showed and cleared only the shell's settings while saying "everything". - 2026-11-23 · Moni did not expect that clearing data in Quizzard would also clear TeamSync / GG Counter, nor that Quizzard's build depended on two other repos; he chose one repo with its own code and its own data. Rule (my reading): a suite app owns its code and its storage by default — shared storage keys or cross-repo builds between apps are proposed to Moni as an explicit choice with the consequences in plain words (what gets deleted where, what breaks if a repo goes away), never taken as the default.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni on the first Answer sheet (a column of cards that grew with every round): "make it like an app with one screen, not a website with a lot of scrolling". Rule (my reading): a tool screen is laid out as an app screen from the start — it fits the viewport (`100dvh` minus the frame), fixed parts stay put, at most one inner area scrolls, and collections that grow (rounds, pages) become tabs or a pager, never a longer page. He also asked for a per-group default (points per round): when items share a value, give the group the setting and let single items override it.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni on desktop, Answer sheet: after 12 rounds the "+" for the next round had scrolled out of the hidden-scrollbar strip, so he could not add more and nothing showed that the strip scrolls; there was no way to delete a line he had added; he asked for cleaner alignment and offsets. Rules (my reading): a strip that scrolls sideways shows arrows at the ends where content is hidden, and the action that adds to it stays outside the strip; every "add" has a visible "remove" (not only a keyboard shortcut); the numbers that add up share one column.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni: the toast after Copy / Delete "stays a long time and is in the way"; he wants it gone much faster or dismissed by a tap elsewhere, "as in other apps". Applied (2.5 s plain, 5 s with Undo, tap elsewhere dismisses). Rule (my reading): feedback toasts are short and never block the next action; the forge's "Undo ≥ 10 s" reads well on paper and is too long on his phone.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni found Home "plain and simple" (a list of rows) and asked for a modern, animated launcher with prettier cards, an opening effect and a simple background. Rule (my reading): a suite's Home is the product's face — it gets a designed layout (tiles, hierarchy, the last-used tool first), entrance and press motion and a screen-change transition from the start, built from tokens and platform APIs only.
- 2026-11-23 · After the first Home redesign Moni said "super" and asked to go bolder: colour, a characterful font, and the same energy inside the tools. Rule (my reading): his taste for his own apps is confident and playful, not minimal-neutral — full-colour surfaces, a display typeface, motion; and a look introduced on one screen is carried through the whole product (per-tool colour by tokens, not by restyling each tool).
- 2026-11-23 · Moni: after opening Teams and going back, the closing animation "always closes the top one" — Home had re-ordered (last used first) while he was away, so the morph landed on a different place. Applied: order fixed per visit. Rule (my reading): an animation that returns to its origin must find the origin where the user left it — never re-order or re-layout a list behind a transition; apply such changes at the next load.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni: "Delete round" in the Answer sheet deleted without asking (only an Undo toast, which is now 5 s). Applied: a confirm when the round has content, Undo kept. Rule (my reading): deleting a whole unit of the user's work (a round, a game, a player with history) asks first; Undo alone is enough only for one small item (a line).
- 2026-11-23 · My mistake: I shipped "ambient" infinite animations on full-screen layers (drifting gradients, twinkling stars) without measuring their cost; they burned a full core and made CI flaky in WebKit, which sent Moni a red e-mail on every push. Rule: no infinite animation on a large or full-screen layer; ambient motion is finite (plays, then rests) or limited to a small element, and its CPU cost is measured (idle Home must be ~0 % CPU) before it ships.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni on 0.14: desktop Home had three tiles in four columns ("strange, make it symmetric"), Scores had 4 cards wider than the panels below, the tools and menus still looked plain next to the new Home, and he asked for a signature opening (logo flies to the header, name to the footer) and "see-through" surfaces. Rule (my reading): (a) every row and every stacked panel on a screen shares the same edges — check each desktop screen for symmetry before shipping; (b) a redesign is applied to every surface at once (Home, tools, menu, dialogs), not Home first; (c) his apps get a signature motion moment whose pieces start and end at real UI places.
- 2026-11-23 · My mistake, second time today: the "glass" brief went to three hands at once and every card and panel got `backdrop-filter`; nobody measured it. CI WebKit crawled and the release stayed off the site. Rule: `backdrop-filter` is budgeted like an infinite animation — at most one blurred surface on screen (the modal backdrop or one floating menu), never on repeated items, never stacked; translucency without blur gives the same look over a soft background. Briefs to subagents state this budget explicitly.
- 2026-11-23 · Third lesson of the day on motion: I brought an endless animation back ("only a small one") after removing them, and CI WebKit went red again on every Home ↔ tool change. Rule: no `infinite` animation in the shell at all — ambient motion plays a few times and rests; and before blaming load, compare the red and green runs for the one thing they differ in.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni waited three hours for 0.15.0 while I pushed guessed fixes for red WebKit runs (animations, blur, fonts) at 15 minutes each; the log had named the cause from the first red run: the app's own error screen (`<main class="es es--screen">` "intercepts pointer events"). Rules: (1) read the full first failure of a red run before changing anything — the call log, not the summary; (2) no second speculative fix: after one miss, add diagnostics and measure; (3) fixes for a browser I cannot run locally are proven on the branch with a manual CI run, never through main; (4) every promise of a browser API that can reject as a normal outcome (`ViewTransition.ready`, `share()`, `play()`, `requestFullscreen()`) gets a handler, and a test that forces that outcome.
- 2026-11-23 · Moni: the splash name grew huge and then snapped small. Cause: the flight scaled onto the full-width row instead of the letters. Rule: an element that flies onto a target must land on a box of the same content size — check the measured widths before and after the flight (they must match), not only a screenshot.
- 2026-11-23 · The scene's pause in tools missed the sun's beams: they turn on ::before/::after, which a `*` selector does not reach (Scores' motion tests caught it). Rule: a rule that stops animations lists `*`, `*::before` and `*::after`; a test counting running animations inside a tool guards it.
- 2026-11-24 · Moni: the stage buttons and the host panel buttons were "completely different" and he had to look for things. Rule: two views of the same thing share one control component (same order, icons, sizes); a counter always says "of how many" (n/total, total dimmer); every new screen gets a walk test that presses every control on phone, tablet, laptop and projector sizes before it ships.
- 2026-11-24 · Moni tapped the plus mark of an empty state: it looked like a button and did nothing. Rule: a decoration never looks like a control — no plus, arrow or pencil in a tile unless it can be pressed. And: state that survives (a saved game with points) is announced when it comes back, with the way to clear it one tap away.
- 2026-11-24 · A CLS test was green here and red on CI twice, and the first fix was a guess (toasts). The difference was the machine: here the fallback font is Inter, close to Manrope, so nothing moved. Rule: before a second guess at a "CI only" failure, list what the CI machine has that this one does not (fonts, screen size, GPU, private-window storage) and reproduce that here — a fontconfig file, the device profile of the failing project in Chromium, a patched API — then fix what the reproduction shows.
- 2026-11-24 · The quiz suites were written and run in Chromium only; five-browser CI then failed 45 tests at once, most from one cause (Blobs in IndexedDB in WebKit) that hid the others. Rule: a new storage path or a new spec file gets one manual five-browser run before the next slice is built on it; test helpers report the browser's own error text, never a fixed "failed".
- 2026-11-24 · Moni: the played quiz was "a bit ugly — it does not look like the main menu, polished, without borderlines". The stage had grown its own look: a 2 px ring (`--qp-edge`) around chips, options, score rows, the answer list and the guide, underlined fields, lined tables. Rule: a new surface takes the app's language from the start — fill + depth (`--qp-lift` / `--quiz-stage-lift` on the stage, `--qp-page-lift` on the host's page), no outline except the focus ring; forced colours get a transparent outline instead
- 2026-11-25 · Moni: the player's Scores dialog was "wooden" — grey page-theme boxes over the violet stage. Everything that opens over the stage (dialogs, host panel) takes the stage's look, not the page theme. Also: a decorative field with a fixed pixel width (stars, 2600 px) must follow the screen width.
- 2026-11-25 · Moni: the player's notes with a button stood 10 s — every note goes after about 5 s and on a tap elsewhere, in the player as in the shell. And: a new part on a slide (the picture with the answer) is looked at with the longest real content (3-line question, long answer, 450-character text) before it ships — a grid row that may shrink to nothing hides overflow from the slide's fit.
- 2026-11-25 · Moni (second time on the answer screen): overlap again with HIS content — a 46-character answer beside a picture. A text that can be long gets a size step by its length (question, answer, explanation), and a grid row that holds it must be able to grow (`1fr`, never `minmax(<length>, 1fr)`). Labels like "Answer" belong at the top of the slide, not between its parts.
- 2026-11-28 · Moni, after the first live test of "Make a quiz with AI": the assistant's answer had a sentence, the code and a closing line — a person does not know which part to copy. What must be copied goes in ONE block with its own Copy button and nothing else competes with it; the instructions say so to the assistant, and the app's own words name that button.
